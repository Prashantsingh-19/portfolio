# Why hire Prashant Singh

## Why hire Prashant: he turns analysis into decisions
Prashant's projects don't stop at a model score. Each ends in a decision someone could act on: which credit applicants to decline and at what cost cut-off, which customer segment to protect first, where to launch a dark store, which tickets to escalate. That is the core of what he brings: the ability to structure a vague business problem, analyse it properly and turn it into a clear recommendation.

## Why hire Prashant: product and analytics together
As a Product Management Intern at i3digital health, Prashant owned an oncology AI product end to end with an engineering team, ran discovery across 10+ competitors, wrote a 5-use-case PRD and built a data pipeline himself. That mix lets him work with both business stakeholders and engineers: he can define what to build and why, and also build the analysis or prototype that proves it.

## Why hire Prashant: rigour and honesty with data
Prashant's physics background shows in how he works: first-principles reasoning, testing ideas against data and reporting what the data says. His projects report honest results, for example that ARIMA return forecasts only tie a naive average, or that a model's cut-off depends heavily on profit assumptions. In his AI products, code computes every number and a validator blocks made-up figures. He would rather be right than impressive.

## Why hire Prashant: range and learning speed
Prashant has built projects across credit risk, fraud detection, market volatility, customer analytics, operations, market sizing and Gen AI products, learning each domain's tools as he went. He picks up new tools quickly and improves with each iteration; Ari, the assistant on his portfolio, is one example of a system he designed and shipped himself.

## What drives Prashant, and a real weakness
Challenges excite Prashant and problems intrigue him. His motto is "any problem is meant to be solved, it's just a question of when." His honest weakness is persistence: he finds it hard to let a problem go before reaching a genuine conclusion rather than a surface-level one. It means real follow-through, but he is learning to judge when a problem is solved well enough to move on.
