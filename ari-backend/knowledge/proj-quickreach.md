# QuickReach — Quick-Commerce Expansion into Tier-2 India

## QuickReach: what it is
QuickReach is a completed product-strategy project Prashant built in October 2026. It answers one question: should a quick-commerce company (like Blinkit, Zepto or Instamart) expand into India's tier-2 cities, and if so, which cities first, and can a dark store break even there? It is a bottom-up market model in Excel covering 10 cities: Jaipur, Lucknow, Kanpur, Nagpur, Indore, Coimbatore, Patna, Bhopal, Vadodara and Ludhiana.

## QuickReach: method and data
The QuickReach funnel runs from households to digitally active households to quick-commerce adopters (scaled by each city's affluence), then orders per month, then annual gross order value (GOV). Real data: Census 2011 city populations projected to 2026, HCES 2023-24 state urban spending from MoSPI, and TRAI urban internet density. Labelled assumptions are anchored to cited benchmarks: adoption (tier-2 e-grocery penetration of about 11%), order frequency (metro users order about 6 times a month, per Redseer) and order value (Blinkit net AOV of about ₹525). Every assumption has Low, Base and High values, and the model includes a scenario switch, a sensitivity grid (adoption × AOV), dark-store payback and city scoring.

## QuickReach: results
In the QuickReach base scenario, the Year-1 market is about ₹980 Cr across the 10 cities, ranging from ₹306 Cr (low) to ₹2,476 Cr (high). A new entrant with 25% share would see about ₹245 Cr GOV. A dark store breaks even at about 705 orders per day. The recommended Wave-1 launch cities are Coimbatore, Nagpur and Jaipur, chosen by a weighted score on market size (50%), affluence (30%) and store headroom (20%). These are model outputs built on public data and labelled assumptions, not observed sales.

## QuickReach: segmentation and positioning
QuickReach defines four personas: young working professionals and couples, students and bachelors, nuclear families doing top-up shopping, and large or joint families who order occasionally. Working professionals and nuclear families drive about 80% of GOV. The positioning thesis: lead with high-frequency top-up categories (dairy, fruit and vegetables, snacks), be the "forgot-something" and late-night option, and don't compete with kirana stores on monthly staples. A launch KPI dashboard tracks orders per store per day, AOV, payback months and repeat rate. Tools: Excel (formula-driven, scenario-linked) and Python (openpyxl).
