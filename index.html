About

Why a physics graduate builds data products

You're probably wondering why a physics graduate is building dashboards and data products. Here is the short version of a calculated, and genuinely enjoyable, journey.

I fell in love with physics while preparing for engineering entrance exams, so much that, after clearing a few colleges, I dropped engineering altogether and pursued a B.Sc. in Physics instead. Which raises the obvious question: why an MBA, and not an M.Sc. or a PhD?

What drew me to physics is how it lets you imagine: how a moving electron generates a magnetic field, how matter shows a dual nature. Imagination has no bounds, but physics adds rules and constraints that make an idea hold up in the real world. Imagine freely, then test against reality. That is the habit I wanted to keep.

With a foundation in first principles, I wanted to point that thinking at something other than machines. Could it create real impact in business? That question brought me to an MBA in Business Analytics at BITS Pilani.

The best way to find out was to start doing it. The projects below are the result.

Risk analytics

Credit scorecards (WoE/IV), default prediction and risk tiering, cost-based cut-offs, and explainable fraud detection with XGBoost and SHAP.

Markets & volatility

GARCH and GJR-GARCH, VaR and Expected Shortfall, Monte Carlo simulation, portfolio optimisation and forecast evaluation against simple benchmarks.

Product & data

Discovery, PRDs and use cases, privacy-by-design data models, Python and SQL pipelines, and dashboards in Power BI, Tableau and Excel.
